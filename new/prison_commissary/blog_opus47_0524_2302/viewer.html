The Spread

6The Same Ramen, Four Times the Price

7The same packet of ramen costs 8$0.24 9in a North Carolina prison and 10$1.06 in Florida — more than four times as much for an identical product. 12For hygiene basics the gap is wider still: deodorant runs six cents in Ohio and $2.90 in Alaska, a 48-fold spread, and toothpaste stretches from $0.12 to $3.67.

17This is not one strange outlier. 18Ranked across all 46 states that opened their books to The Appeal, ramen climbs in a smooth gradient from North Carolina's 24 cents to Florida's lone dollar-plus packet, with a national median of just 47 cents.

19Outside prison, that same packet costs about 30 to 35 cents at a store like Target. 20Inside, where the commissary is the only shop in town, a captive market sets a price that can run three times higher.

The Wage

23The Exchange Rate Is Human Labor

24To understand these prices you have to know the wage behind them. 25Incarcerated workers earn roughly 14 to 63 cents an hour nationally — and far less in places like Louisiana, which averages six cents, or Texas, which pays nothing at all. 26Many work full days for about six dollars every two weeks.

27At six cents an hour, the median deodorant costs 22 hours of work. 28Toothpaste, 21 hours. 29A jar of peanut butter, 42 hours. 30The numbers stop reading like prices and start reading like sentences.

And an electric fan — not a luxury, but often the only defense against dangerous heat in prisons without air conditioning — costs 32382 hours at that wage. 33That is more than nine full forty-hour weeks of labor for a single fan.

The Heat

45The Price of Staying Cool

46A fan should be the cheapest insurance against a heat wave. 47In prison it is a financial event. 48The cheapest fan ranges from 49$10.02 in Wyoming to 50$43.80 in Nevada — the same 4.4x spread we saw with ramen, only now the stakes are heatstroke.

52Nine states price their cheapest fan above $27; South Dakota, Indiana, Utah, and Georgia all clear $32. 53The Appeal documented fans selling for nearly $40 at one Delaware facility and around $33 in Indiana, in buildings that routinely bake through the summer.

The Turn

56What Costs the Most Is Faith

57Rank all 24 products by price and the cheapest things are the ones a prison cannot really withhold: a toothbrush at 42 cents, ramen at 47, soap at 66. 58The most expensive things are the items tied to faith — and a fan.

8 59$25.99 vs $5.21 median price of a Quran versus a Bible — nearly five times more for the central text of Islam

61A Quran has a median price of 62$25.99; a Bible, 63$5.21 — nearly five times less for the central text of Christianity than for the central text of Islam. 65Connecticut's list showed it starkly: a Bible for $4.55, the Noble Quran for $25.99. 66The Star of David and the crucifix both run above $25, while Christian rosary beads cost under two dollars.

67Prayer rugs ($15.77 median) and hijabs ($12.27) pile further cost onto Islamic practice. 68Religious access is a protected right, but in the commissary the financial barrier to practicing it falls hardest on Muslims and Jews.

The Market

Who Sets the Price

72So who decides what a fan or a Quran costs? 73Of the 46 states, 17 run their commissaries themselves, but 29 hand the store to a private vendor. 74Keefe Commissary alone supplies 16 states. 75These contracts typically pay the state a commission on every sale — so the prison earns more when the prices go up, not down.

78The result is a geography of cost. 79The same basket of needs averages 80$8.36 81per item in Indiana and just 82$0.93 in Michigan — nine times more in one state than another. 84Where a person is incarcerated, far more than what they buy, decides what survival costs.
