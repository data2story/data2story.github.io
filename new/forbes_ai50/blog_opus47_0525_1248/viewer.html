Fifty companies. 3Almost all the money belongs to two.

4Add up every dollar raised by the fifty most promising private AI companies in the world and you get about $305.7 billion. 5Then notice that two of them — OpenAI and Anthropic — hold $242.6 billion of it. Two companies. 679.4% of the money.

7The other forty-eight split what's left. 8The median company on this list has raised $735 million; the average has raised $6.1 billion. 9When the mean is more than eight times the median, you are not looking at a leaderboard — you are looking at a power law.

10This is what an AI gold rush looks like from the capital side: a vanishingly small number of foundation-model labs absorbing money at a scale venture capital has never seen, while everyone else operates one or two orders of magnitude below them.

Geography

35One city, half the list

36If the money is concentrated, the geography is almost comic. 37Exactly half the entire list — 25 of 50 companies — is headquartered in the city of San Francisco. 38Not the Bay Area, not California: the city itself.

39Widen to the rest of California and you reach 33 of 50, two-thirds of the list. 40Only eleven companies are anywhere else in the United States, and just six are international. 41One city holds as many AI-50 companies as the rest of the world and the rest of America put together.

42The international tail is thin enough to name in a breath: Cohere in Toronto, Mistral in Paris, Black Forest Labs in Freiburg, Synthesia in London, and two Swedish companies — Legora and Lovable — both in Stockholm. 43No company on the list is headquartered in Asia.

Vintage

Born yesterday

47These are not seasoned institutions. 48Sixty-eight percent of the list was founded in 2020 or later, and 56% in 2022 or later — after ChatGPT's late-2022 debut lit the fuse. 49The single busiest founding year is 2022 with eleven companies, trailed by 2023 with ten. 50Those two years alone produced 21 of the 50.

51The oldest company here, Databricks, dates to 2013. 52The newest, Thinking Machines Lab, was founded in 2025 — and had already raised $2 billion in time to make the list.

53Age buys you nothing in this market. 54The correlation between founding year and funding is slightly negative, and the 2022–2025 cohort's median raise ($668 million) sits within reach of companies twice their age. 55Capital chases perceived frontier potential, not seniority.

The turn

59A broad field, a narrow purse

60By headcount, the AI 50 is mostly applied AI, not labs. 61The largest single category is generative media — eight companies turning text into images, video, voice and music. 62Foundation models and AI infrastructure tie for second at seven each. 63Then come agents, coding tools, robotics, healthcare, legal, and data labeling.

64The descriptions themselves carry the 2025 zeitgeist: five companies are built around "agents," eight around "generation," six around coding and app-building. 65The verbs have shifted from answering to doing.

Caveats

87What the list can't tell you

88Read all of this as a careful sketch, not a ledger. 89The AI 50 is a curated editorial list of private companies only — chosen by Forbes with Sequoia and Meritech — so it omits every public AI leader and any private company the algorithm didn't surface. 90Nvidia, Google and Microsoft are nowhere on it by design.

91The funding figures are rounded and frozen in time: Anthropic's "$60 billion" was already climbing past $180 billion in valuation within months, and two companies — Midjourney and Surge AI — read "$0" not because they have no money but because they never disclosed it or never needed it.

92So the numbers are a snapshot of a wave mid-break: the concentration is real, the youth is real, the California gravity is real. 93But a list this top-heavy and this recent is, above all, a bet on a future that hadn't arrived yet when the data was frozen.
