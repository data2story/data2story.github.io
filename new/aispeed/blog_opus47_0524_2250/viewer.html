1AI Model Iteration Speed

Every four days

2The frontier labs once shipped a new language model every few months. 3Now it is every few days. 4This is what that acceleration looks like — in days.

5In 2018, the world's leading AI labs shipped a notable new language model about once every 132 days — roughly every four-and-a-half months. 6By 2025, the median gap between consecutive frontier releases was four days. 7That is a 33-fold acceleration in seven years.

9The pace is now fast enough to break the institutions built around it. 10Trackers report the three leading labs updating their flagship models every few weeks rather than every few months, and organizations that once ran six-month evaluation cycles are being forced onto four-week ones — because the top model can change two or three times inside a single quarter.

11This is the story of a clock speeding up. 12Not the models getting smarter, which is a different story — but the raw cadence of release, measured in days.

28The cliff was 2022

29The acceleration did not creep. 30From 2019 to 2021 the median gap held steady between 49 and 68 days. 31Then it fell off a cliff: 13 days in 2022, 5.5 days in 2023. 32In two years the interval compressed nearly ninefold.

33The cliff lands exactly on the ChatGPT year. 34ChatGPT launched in November 2022 and reached 100 million users in two months, triggering Google's internal “code red,” a $10 billion Microsoft bet on OpenAI, and Meta's decision to open-source Llama. 35The release log shows that competitive panic as a near-vertical drop.

36And it was the whole distribution that moved, not a few outliers. 37In 2019 the average gap (85 days) sat far above the median (51), dragged up by long lulls. 38By 2025 the two had converged — a mean of under seven days against a median of four. 39The quiet stretches simply disappeared.

42Eighteen times the releases

43The mirror image of shrinking gaps is exploding volume. 44The frontier labs counted three language-model releases in 2018. In 2025 they counted 54 — eighteen times as many. 46The climb tracks the same inflection: 8 in 2021, 21 in 2022, 33 in 2024.

47Some of that surge is more labs entering the race, and some is point-releases — the 4.1s, the 4.5s, the “Pro” and “Heavy” and “Thinking” variants — each clearing the notability bar as a separate entry. 48That is not a measurement artifact so much as the phenomenon itself: labs now ship smaller, more frequent increments. 49A release in 2025 is a smaller step than a release in 2019, taken far more often.

52Nobody is sitting still

53This is not one hyperactive lab dragging an average down. 54Every frontier lab tightened its own cadence. 55Measured on each lab's own release-to-release gaps, OpenAI is the pace-setter at a 21-day median, followed by Alibaba at 36 and Google DeepMind at 58; even the slower-cadence labs like Mistral and xAI are constrained mostly by how recently they entered the race.

56OpenAI's own compression is the clearest microcosm: its median gap between releases fell from 108 days through 2022 to just 17 days from 2024 onward — a sixfold tightening inside a single organization.

59From seasons to weeks

60Read one lab's release log and you can feel the clock change. 61Anthropic's Claude 2 arrived 119 days after the prior release; Claude 2.1 another 133 days later — seasonal cadence. 62By late 2025 the gaps were measured in weeks: Claude Sonnet 4.5, then Claude Haiku 4.5 just 16 days behind it, then Claude Opus 4.5 forty days after that.

63Twice, the gap hit zero. 64Claude 3 Opus and Claude 3 Sonnet shipped on the same day; so did Opus 4 and Sonnet 4. A “release” stopped being a single model and became a simultaneous family.

69When the gap hits zero

70That simultaneity is its own signal. 71Across the dataset, 25 releases have a gap of zero days — a lab shipping two notable models on the same date. 72OpenAI accounts for 11 of them. 73And they cluster in the present: eight same-day launches in 2025 alone, against three or four a year in 2022–2024 and essentially none before 2019.

74When a lab's gap between its own releases hits zero, the cadence has effectively maxed out. 75There is no faster than the same day. 76The frontier is no longer racing to be next; in stretches, it is arriving all at once.

79The clock that won't slow

Four days. That is the interval the frontier now keeps — faster than most teams can read a model card, run an eval, and write up the results.

81The pace has become a barrier to entry in its own right: keeping up is now part of what it means to be a frontier lab. 82The race is no longer only about who builds the best model. 83It is about who can keep the drumbeat going.
