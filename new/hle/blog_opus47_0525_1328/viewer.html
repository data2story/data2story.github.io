The Test

5Can You Even Understand the Question?

6In early 2025, researchers gave the world's best AI models a new exam. 7GPT-4o scored 2.7%. 8Claude 3.5 Sonnet managed 4.1%. 9OpenAI's o1 — a model built specifically to reason — reached 8.0%. 10On most modern benchmarks these systems score above 90%. 11This one was different by design.

13It is called Humanity's Last Exam, and the fastest way to understand it is not to read about it. 14It is to try to answer one of its questions yourself.

Why It Exists

19The Test Built to Be Failed

20HLE exists because our rulers for measuring AI stopped working. 21Large language models now score over 90% on popular benchmarks like MMLU, which makes those tests useless for telling one frontier model from another. 22Dan Hendrycks, who directs the Center for AI Safety, started the project after Elon Musk remarked that existing benchmarks were too easy.

23The answer was to build the hardest closed-ended academic test that humans could still write and grade. 24The Center for AI Safety and Scale AI assembled 2,500 questions across more than 100 subjects, contributed by nearly 1,000 subject-matter experts from over 500 institutions in 50 countries. 25Every question has a single, verifiable answer that cannot be found with a quick search.

By Design

3270,000 Attempts, 13,000 Survivors

33The hardness is not luck. 34Experts competed for a $500,000 prize pool, and every submission was first thrown at frontier models. 35Only questions the models failed — or answered worse than random guessing — advanced. 36More than 70,000 model-evaluation attempts winnowed the pool to about 13,000 stumpers, which then passed two rounds of graduate-level human review before the final 2,500 were chosen.

45The Shape of the Data

46A Math Exam With Seven Appendices

47In headcount terms, HLE is a math exam. 48The 1,021 mathematics questions are 40.8% of the whole thing — 3.6 times the next-largest category. 49The other seven categories together make up the remaining 59.2%.

50That lopsidedness is not an artifact of this snapshot: the category shares here match the figures HLE's creators publish almost exactly, none deviating by more than about a percentage point. 51Math 40.8%, Biology/Medicine 11.2%, Computer Science/AI 9.6%, then a cluster of mid-sized fields down to Engineering at 4.4%.

The Breadth

56193 Subjects, 579 Experts

57Beneath the eight tidy categories sit 193 distinct subjects, and they are wildly uneven. 58"Mathematics" alone is 863 questions (34.5% of the exam). 59But the tail is enormous: 147 subjects appear five times or fewer, and 83 subjects appear exactly once.

63The people behind them tell the same story. 64The 2,109 attributed questions come from 579 named contributors. 65The most prolific wrote 99; but the top ten together account for only 22.1% of attributed questions, and fully half of all named authors (289 of 579) contributed exactly one. 66HLE is built on breadth, not on a handful of power users.

Anatomy

73The Shape of an Impossible Question

74Three-quarters of the exam (76.4%) is short-answer, not multiple choice — and short does not mean easy. 75Among those 1,909 exact-match questions the median answer is just 8 characters, and almost a third are three characters or fewer: a single number, letter or symbol. 76The answer is tiny and unforgiving; being one digit off scores zero.

The Aftermath

Scaling the Wall

92HLE was built to last, but AI moves fast. 93Within about a year the scores climbed from the single digits into the mid-40s: as of May 2026 Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview leads at about 44.7%, GPT-5.4 at 41.6%. 94That is a stunning jump from o3-mini's 13.4% launch-day high — though it still leaves more than half the exam unsolved, and these tool-augmented figures are not directly comparable to the no-tools launch numbers.

100And the wall has cracks. 101In July 2025 the AI-science lab FutureHouse audited the text-only chemistry and biology questions and estimated that about 30% of those answers conflicted with the published literature; the HLE team ran its own checks and put the error rate nearer 18%. 102Either way, the lesson holds: the answers here are expert-reviewed but not infallible, and the subject labels are contributor-assigned. 103Treat HLE as a remarkable, fast-aging human artifact — not as ground truth.

105What makes Humanity's Last Exam worth your attention is exactly this doubleness. 106It is the most ambitious attempt yet to measure machine knowledge against the human frontier, assembled from a thousand specialists' hardest questions — and it is already being outrun, one corrected answer at a time.
