§ One year, the whole story

8Spotlight, ranked twenty-fifth

9In 2015, the Academy gave Best Picture to Spotlight. The same year, IMDb users — quoting, parodying, citing one film inside another — added 358 references to 11Star Wars: The Force Awakens12, 157 to 13Fifty Shades of Grey14, and 133 to 15Avengers: Age of Ultron. Spotlight 16picked up 31. 17That puts it at rank twenty-five of all 2015 films by reference count: the worst rank ever held by a 21st-century Best Picture winner.

Spotlight is not an outlier. It is the median. 18From oscars_year 2001 to 2016, no Best Picture winner has been the most-referenced film of its own year. 19The last winner to top its own year was Gladiator 20in 2000.

§ How we are measuring

26What an IMDb 'connection' is

27IMDb keeps a crowdsourced ledger called "movie connections": a record kept whenever one title quotes, parodies, samples, or visually nods to another. A Casablanca 28poster on a character's wall counts. 29The famous Psycho strings being parodied counts. A passing joke about "Bond films" does not — IMDb only logs a connection when the filmmakers clearly had a specific other title in mind. 31The dataset compiled by The Economist 32counts these connections for 27,826 unique films; a 2018 paper by Bioglio and Pensa at the University of Turin used the same source to build a citation network across 47,000 films and rank cinema's most influential works.

33The obvious objection is that older films have had more time to accumulate references. 34The dataset answers this by computing each film's annual share35: its references as a percentage of all references to films released the same year. Casablanca is Casablanca 36not because 1943 has had eight extra decades, but because 48.7% of every IMDb reference to a 1943 film points back to it.

§ Eight decades of one number

38From 9% to 1.5%

39The average Best Picture winner used to occupy a much bigger piece of its year. 40In the 1940s the mean annual_share for winners was 7.7%. In the 1970s — Patton, Godfather, Rocky, all of which finished #1 in their year — it peaked at 9.4%. 42By the 2010s it has fallen to 1.5%. 43Median rank has moved with it: a 1970s winner sat, on average, at rank three or four of its year; a 2010s winner sits at rank eight.

§ When the snub is bigger than the prize

The 18.6× snub

57In 1980, Best Picture went to Ordinary People58, a Robert Redford-directed family drama that has accumulated 94 IMDb references. That same year, 59The Empire Strikes Back — never nominated for Best Picture — accumulated 1,748. 61The snubbed film is referenced 18.6 times more often. 62It is the largest gap of its kind in the dataset.

Three years earlier Annie Hall beat Star Wars63, which received 18.1 times more references; in 1960 The Apartment beat Psycho (15.9×); in 1968 Oliver! beat 652001: A Space Odyssey (9.0×); in 1994 Forrest Gump beat Pulp Fiction (1.7×); in 2008 Slumdog Millionaire finished above The Dark Knight (3.0× in our data); in 2016 Moonlight beat 69Batman v Superman: Dawn of Justice (5.9×).

71In 74 of the 89 years that produced a Best Picture winner, at least one film that was not even nominated has more lifetime references than the actual winner. 72The data does not say the Academy is wrong. 73It says it is consistently choosing a different prize than the one the culture, in aggregate, hands out.

§ The parallel canon

79The People's Best Pictures

80If the year's most-referenced film were the year's "people's pick", the 2001-2016 winners list would read: 81The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring, Spider-Man, 82Pirates of the Caribbean, Saw, 83Star Wars: Revenge of the Sith, 300, Transformers, Twilight, Avatar, Inception, Thor, The Avengers, Frozen, 84Guardians of the Galaxy, The Force Awakens, Batman v Superman. 85Eleven of those sixteen were never nominated for Best Picture. Three were — Fellowship, Avatar, Inception — and only Fellowship's sequel Return of the King eventually won.

86You can argue these films do not deserve Best Picture; that is exactly the argument the Academy is making by not nominating them. 87The point is narrower: those 88are the films other films keep reaching for. 89Anyone making a movie in 2024 is more likely to quote, riff on, or homage Inception or The Force Awakens than they are The King's Speech or Spotlight. 90The IMDb ledger is just keeping count.

§ Where prize and culture aligned

96The peaks the Academy got right

97When the Academy and the culture do agree, the reading is unambiguous. Casablanca 98won Best Picture in 1943 and now owns 48.7% of every reference to every film released that year — a single picture absorbing nearly half of its cohort's downstream cultural energy. 99The next-most-referenced film of 1943 is Lassie Come Home100, at 4.8%.

101Casablanca's share is unmatched, but the pattern shows up across the canon: The Godfather 102(33.7% of 1972), Titanic 103(17.2% of 1997), Rocky 104(19.1% of 1976), The Sound of Music 105(23.7% of 1965), Lawrence of Arabia 106(12.1% of 1962). 107When a Best Picture winner does anchor its year, it tends to anchor it dramatically. 108The most-referenced film in the entire dataset, however, is 109Star Wars: A New Hope (4,965 references, 55% of 1977) — and it lost to Annie Hall.

§ A different kind of Best Picture

118Three numbers from one dataset

119Three numbers, all from the same dataset. 120Before 2001, eleven of fifty-two Best Picture winners (21%) were the most-referenced film of their year. 121After 2000, that figure is zero through 2016. 122The mean annual share of a winner has fallen by 6.4× from its 1970s peak.

123None of this proves the Academy's choices are bad films. Spotlight, Moonlight, Birdman, 12412 Years a Slave are serious work. 125But Best Picture used to be, on average, the year's centre of gravity — the movie everyone else's movies referred to. It is not, anymore. The references — for better or worse — have left the building.
