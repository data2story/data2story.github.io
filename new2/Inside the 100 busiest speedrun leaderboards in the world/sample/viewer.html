22None of this is the picture casual observers carry around. 23The intuition is that the busiest speedrun leaderboards must be the most-iconic games — the SM64s, the GTAs, the Hollow Knights. 24The actual picture is something different: a working snapshot of where dedicated communities happen to be pouring labour at this particular moment. 25That is what the rest of this piece is about.

28What "most active" actually selects

29The dataset is the 100 games on speedrun.com with the most-recently-verified runs as of 25 May 2026: 61,797 runs across 608 per-game categories, submitted by 29,548 distinct registered runners. 30A "game" on speedrun.com is not one leaderboard but a small constellation of them — the median game in this cut carries five separately-ranked categories (Any%, 100%, Glitchless, and so on), the maximum is 31 (Super Mario Bros. 31Category Extensions). 3219 games carry 10 or more parallel leaderboards.

33Monthly verification volume across these 100 boards has risen roughly tenfold since 2020. 34The 2020 baseline was 155-500 verifications per month. 35By 2026, the typical month is 1,800-3,150. 36The single largest month in the dataset's history is March 2026, with 3,151 verifications. 37Speedrun.com was founded by Irish programmer Peter Chase ("Pac") in March 2014 with deliberately looser rules than the older Speed Demos Archive; by mid-2021 it hosted over 20,000 game leaderboards. 38The 100 boards in our cut are the freshest tip of that iceberg.

39"Currently most-active" rewards three populations of leaderboards. 40The first is brand-new releases still onboarding their first runners — Subnautica 2 launched into Early Access on 14 May 2026 and arrived in the top-100 with 57 verified runs in eleven days. 41The second is recent blockbusters where the meta is still being figured out — Hollow Knight: Silksong, Hades 2. 42The third is "evergreen grinders" — Granny: Legacy, the SpongeBob series, ROBLOX-engine titles — where a small dedicated cohort uploads runs at a steady rate. 43The canon (SM64, Half-Life, Undertale) is present too, but as second-rank presences rather than the top of the list.

The megalaunch arc

49Hollow Knight: Silksong was announced 14 February 2019 and released 4 September 2025 — a wait of six and a half years, during which Team Cherry's near-silence became its own joke. 50It sold over 7 million copies by mid-December 2025 and broke 500,000 concurrent Steam players on launch day. 51The speedrun leaderboards opened 1 October 2025. 52Within a month, per PC Gamer, Silksong had "more active runners than every other game on Speedrun.com, including Celeste and ULTRAKILL."

53That spike is visible in the data: the Any% category took in 81 verified runs in its first week, held above 40 runs per week for the next six weeks, then settled into a 7-30 runs/week pattern through winter 2025-26. 54The volume curve is a classic speedrun-launch shape — a roaring early burst as everyone tries the new game, then a slow settling as the route stabilises and only the dedicated stay.

55But there is a second curve underneath the first, and it is the more interesting one. 56In Silksong's launch week the median Any% submission was 4,975 seconds — 82.9 minutes. 57By 10 November, the weekly median had dropped to 68.4 minutes. 58By 2 February 2026, 61.4 minutes. 59By 20 April, 58.4 minutes. 60Across eight months the typical run shrank by about 22 minutes — a 27% reduction in median time. 61The route is now well-understood. 62The community as a whole, not just the world-record holder, has internalised the optimisation.

63The standout name in the dataset's Silksong roster is Blue, a Swedish runner whose best Any% time across four submissions is 3,422 seconds: 57 minutes and 2 seconds. 64External reporting from late 2025 had Blue's world record at 1:03:41. 65The version in our dataset is six minutes faster. 66It is the only sub-60-minute Silksong Any% run held by any of the game's top 15 most prolific runners.

67Silksong's runner base is also unusually international for a US-developer-led release. 68The USA leads with 292 of 1,096 distinct runners (27%) — but France is a strong second at 83, China third at 59, and Brazil, Germany, Australia, and the UK all sit between 30 and 50. 69The global average puts the USA at 31% of runners; Silksong sits below that.

The grinder dynasty

77If Silksong is the announced megalaunch, Granny: Legacy 78is the unannounced one. 79Its leaderboard opened in February 2025 with a single verified run. 80By April 2025 it was at 12 runs per month, by July 28, by December 292. 81In January 2026 the leaderboard saw 501 verifications. 82By April it was at 899. 83The first three weeks of May 2026 had already produced 475. 84That is one of the steepest growth curves of any speedrun community in our dataset — from one run/month to ~900 runs/month in fifteen months — and it is still climbing.

85The other thing about Granny: Legacy is 86where its runners are from. 87Of 1,245 distinct runners with a stated country, 533 (42.8%) are from Russia. 88Add Ukraine (142), Belarus (48), and Kazakhstan (47) and four post-Soviet states account for 770 of 1,245 runners — 8961.8% of the entire Granny: Legacy community. 90The next-largest country (Romania, 63 runners) is more than 8x smaller than Russia alone. 91The USA, which dominates almost every other leaderboard in the dataset at 30%+, contributes 27 runners. 92No other game in the top-100 has anything close to this kind of single-region capture.

93Granny is not alone in being a single-game dynasty. 94The all-time peak month for verification activity (3,151 runs in March 2026) was driven 33.8% by SpongeBob SquarePants: Battle for Bikini Bottom and 24.2% by Granny: Legacy — two games accounting for 58% of a single month's activity across the entire top-100 cut. 95SpongeBob's BFBB community has been a speedrun anchor since AGDQ 2017 and has grown into a series of eight separate leaderboards in the top-100. 96These dynasties make most of the noise.

10192% of speedrunners only run one game

102The other side of the labour market is the runners themselves. 103Of the 28,069 distinct registered runners in the dataset, 10425,863 (92.1%) submitted verified runs in only one game. 105Another 1,758 (6.3%) cover two games. 106439 (1.6%) cover three to five. 107Only nine runners — barely 0.03% of the population — cover six or more. 108The median runner has run one game, once. 109The mean is 1.10 games per runner. 110Speedrunning, even at the active edge, is a community of monogamists.

113The same skew shows up in run counts. 114The top 1% of registered runners (280 people) account for 16.5% of all verified runs in the dataset. 115The top 5% account for 33.7%. 116The top 10% account for 44.6%. 117The top 20% account for 57.7% — closer to a 60/20 distribution than to the classic 80/20. 118The bottom half of the runner population contributes 23.2% combined. 119This is what a labour market with a long, long tail looks like.

132Where the records actually live

133If you ask which country produces the most speedrunners in this cut, the answer is unsurprising: the United States, with 8,031 of 25,580 runners (31.4%). 134The follow-up — Russia 6.7%, Canada 4.7%, France 4.2%, Germany 3.6%, Brazil 3.2% — looks like the global map of any online community.

135But ask a different question — "where do the world records 136live?" — and the map rearranges. 137Take all 1,210 leaderboard rank-1 rows (one per category) and look at the runner's country. 138The USA holds 210 (24.1% of WRs with a known country), meaningfully less 139than its 31% share of runners would predict. 140Russia holds 11.7% of WRs against 6.7% of runners — almost double its share. 141Brazil holds 10.9% of WRs against 3.2% of runners — punching nearly 3.5x its weight. 142Czech Republic (6.4% of WRs, 0.5% of runners) and Romania (6.0% of WRs, 0.7% of runners) appear to be entirely different countries on the WR map than on the runner map. 143Both punch around 10x their share.

144Put bluntly: the easy thing to say about speedrunning is that the USA wins. 145The harder, more interesting thing the data says is that the USA wins by raw numbers but consistently under-performs per-capita-of-runners, while small Eastern European countries pull WRs at multiples of their effective participation rate.

149The platforms beneath the runs

150Roughly half of all runs in our top-100 cut were performed on PC — 30,755 of 61,797 (49.8%). 151The other half is spread across 65 distinct platforms, with no single one above 8%. 152Web games account for 7.4% (mostly Google Snake and other browser titles). 153The original Nintendo 64 still appears in 6.7% of runs, almost all of them Super Mario 64. 154Android takes 5.6% (largely Granny). 155The long tail runs from PlayStation 4 down to a single run verified on "Airplane Seats."

156Emulator share — the fraction of runs flagged as performed on an emulator rather than original hardware or PC-native — peaks in 2018 at 18.3% of that year's runs. 157It has been declining since: ~10% across 2020-2024, 8.5% in 2025, 7.8% so far in 2026. 158Part of this is composition (more PC-native games entering the most-active cut, with no emulator distinction to make); part is real (tightening rulesets on classic-console leaderboards, plus easier and cheaper access to original hardware). 159The verification system on speedrun.com is decentralised to each game's moderator team, so this is the aggregate effect of dozens of independent rule choices.

163Drill into one canonical retro title and the picture is sharper. 164Super Mario 64 has 4,403 verified runs in the dataset; 3,997 of them (90.8%) are on the original Nintendo 64 platform — and within that N64 cohort, 16540.4% are flagged as emulated. 166"SM64 on N64" today is roughly a 60/40 split between original cartridge hardware and software emulators. 167The 9% of runs on Wii or Switch Virtual Console are themselves running emulated copies, just official ones. 168Calling any of this "console speedrunning" requires a footnote.

173None of these patterns are permanent. 174The dataset is a snapshot of late May 2026. 175Run the same fetch in November 2026 and Granny: Legacy will have either matured into a stable community or collapsed; Silksong will either still be receiving routes or be a dormant leaderboard; some game we haven't named will be the next surprise. 176The 100 most-active boards are a moving window onto a working community — and the only honest way to read the snapshot is as a labour-market document, not as a verdict on who is the best at video games.
