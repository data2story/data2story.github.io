How far is too far?

2In 2017, the nearest abortion clinic was functionally next door at 8 weeks of pregnancy and a day's drive away at 20 — and the closure of a single clinic moved the country from the first picture toward the second.

How far is too far?

3At 8 weeks of pregnancy — when 9 in 10 US abortions take place — 151 of the 749 large cities in this dataset record a round-trip drive of an hour or more to the nearest abortion clinic. 4By 20 weeks, that figure is 339 cities — nearly half.

5The population-weighted round-trip drive grows from 15 minutes at 8 weeks to over an hour at 20. 6The share of urban Americans facing a 4-hour-plus round-trip rises from 1.4% to 10.8%. 7The geography of access does not stand still as a pregnancy progresses; it collapses.

8In September 2017 The Pudding published a map. 9This is what was on it.

17Eight weeks: the friendly map

18At 8 weeks of gestation, 598 of 749 cities — 79.8% — record a round-trip drive of zero hours. 19The closest clinic sits in or immediately next to the city. 20Only 14 cities (1.9%) face a round-trip drive of four hours or more. 21Population-weighted, 88.8% of the urban Americans in this dataset live in a 0-hour-drive city.

28Sixteen weeks: the map breaks

29In Boise, Idaho, the round-trip drive to the nearest clinic is 0 hours at 8 weeks. 30At 16 weeks it becomes 8 hours. 31Three other Idaho cities — Nampa, Meridian, Caldwell — show the identical pattern. 32So does El Paso, Texas (0 → 7) and Bend, Oregon (0 → 6). 33For these cities the gestational ladder is binary: a first-trimester patient never leaves town; a second-trimester patient drives across or out of the state.

35The same collapse shows up at the population level. 36The share of urban Americans facing a 4-hour-plus round-trip more than triples between 8 and 20 weeks (1.4% → 10.8%). 37The clinic universe for a 20-week patient is a small fraction of the universe for an 8-week one — and the geography does the rest.

38Where the ladder ends

39Population-weighted across each state's qualifying cities, Idaho records a 20-week round-trip drive of 9.0 hours — the longest in the dataset. 40North Dakota follows at 8.6, Louisiana at 6.6, South Dakota at 6.5, Mississippi at 5.3.

41Idaho also posts the largest 8-to-20-week growth in any state — a population-weighted 8.4-hour increase. 42North Dakota grows 6.7, Louisiana 6.2, Mississippi 4.8, Virginia 4.3. 43These are not all remote states: Virginia is the 12th most populous in the country.

44By contrast, 18 states record a population-weighted 20-week drive under one hour. 45The 27 "abortion deserts" identified by ANSIRH researchers in 2017 — large cities more than 100 miles from a clinic — sit, almost without exception, inside the same state cluster.

50Eleven hours from Bismarck

51Some cities never had a near-by option. 52Rapid City, South Dakota records a 9-hour round-trip drive even at 8 weeks. 53Lubbock, Midland, Odessa, Amarillo and San Angelo — all in West Texas — register 6 to 8 hours round-trip at the earliest gestation. 54Their first-trimester patients are already on what is functionally a road trip.

55At 20 weeks, the longest round-trip in the dataset is Bismarck, North Dakota, at 11 hours — the equivalent of a working day plus a meal. 56Lubbock, Boise, Nampa, Meridian, Caldwell, Rapid City and Missoula each record 10-hour round-trips.

59Driving time is the floor of the burden, not the ceiling. 6027 US states required a mandatory waiting period between counseling and procedure in 2017, and 14 of those required the counseling in person — meaning two trips. 61A 10-hour round-trip in that legal context is not "a long drive"; it is two days off work, a hotel, gas, and whatever child care a person has to arrange to disappear for 48 hours.

65What a single closure does

66The dataset's "closed" columns simulate the closure of the city's closest clinic — a controlled stress test on the system. 67The result: the share of cities lacking a 1-hour round-trip jumps from 20.2% to 37.8% at 8 weeks, and from 45.3% to 67.4% at 20 weeks. 68Population-weighted, the share of urban Americans living in a 0-hour-drive city collapses from 66.9% to 42.7% at 20 weeks.

74At 8 weeks, five cities in the Texas Rio Grande Valley — McAllen, Mission, Edinburg, Pharr, Harlingen — go from a 0-1 hour drive to a 6-7 hour drive when their nearest clinic is removed. 75Three North Dakota cities jump 6-7 hours. 76Mississippi's Jackson goes from 0 to 5; Louisiana's Shreveport from 0 to 5. 77These are one-clinic regions.

78At 20 weeks the picture is stranger and starker. 79Salt Lake City, Albuquerque, Billings — all population centers — sit at zero hours in the open scenario and at 11 to 15 hours when a single clinic closes. 80Eight Utah cities move 10-12 hours each. 81The closure of a single second-trimester provider is enough to push hundreds of thousands of urban Americans into a 10-plus-hour round-trip.

92A baseline, not a present

93This dataset documents the access network in a specific window: HB2 had been overturned but its closed clinics had not reopened, and Roe v. 94Wade was still standing. 95The Pudding's headline ("How far is too far?") was an open question.

96Five years after this snapshot, in June 2022, Dobbs v. 97Jackson Women's Health 98overturned Roe and triggered a cascade of total bans and 6-week, 12-week, and 15-week limits across roughly half the country. 99The closed columns of this 2017 dataset turned out to be a dress rehearsal: the cities where a single closure pushed the round-trip into double digits are now the cities whose patients drive to Illinois, New Mexico, Colorado, or Kansas — not because their nearest clinic closed, but because every clinic in their state did.
