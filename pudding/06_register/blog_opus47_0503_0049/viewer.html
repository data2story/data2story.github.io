7A 1.65-point gap that did not exist before

8In 2019, the male-led top-10 of the Billboard Hot 100 averaged a Pandora vocal-register score of 7.60. 9The rest of the year's male-led charting songs — songs that made the Hot 100 but not the Hot 10 — averaged 5.95. 10The gap is 1.65 register points, more than double anything in the previous 60 years.

11That 7.60 is not just a recent peak. 12It is the highest top-10 register year in the entire 1958-2019 series. 13The previous record was 1988 — Bon Jovi, Poison, Whitney Houston-era hair metal — and even 1988 only reached 7.21.

14The all-songs picture tells a completely different story. 15There, 2019 sits at 5.95 — below 1958, half a point below the early-1990s plateau, and 1.13 points below 1990's all-time high of 7.08. 16The shift is not in pop's average. 17It is in pop's ceiling.

21How register is measured (and what it isn't)

22The 1-to-10 score comes from Pandora's Music Genome Project, where human musicologists spend twenty to thirty minutes listening to each song and rate roughly 450 attributes. 23One of those attributes is vocal register: how high the lead voice sounds, on a 1-to-10 ordinal axis.

24On the low end, a 2 sounds like Isaac Hayes growling under "Theme From Shaft" or War's spoken-low "Low Rider". 25On the high end, a 10 is what Ginuwine's "Pony" gets — a near-the-ceiling delivery that became the scale's textbook anchor. 26In between sit 11,944 male-led Hot 100 songs spanning sixty years.

27The score does not separate falsetto from belt. 28A high register reading means the voice sounds high; the technique behind it — a chest-voice belt, a head-voice push, a falsetto leap — is collapsed into one number. 29That is the cost of having sixty consistent years of data and the reason the dataset can talk about pop's sound but not its mechanism.

30Across all 11,944 songs the modal register is 7 (27.7% of the dataset). 31Registers 5, 6, and 7 together cover almost 70% of male-led pop history. 32Register 10 — the very top — is only 1.3% of the dataset. 33Most male pop sits in the comfortable upper-middle of a tenor's range.

39The 1980s were the actual peak

40When you average every charting male-led song instead of just the top 10, the 1980s win on every count. 411990 averages 7.08 — the highest single-year value in the entire series — followed by 1987 (7.0802), 1988 (7.0782), and 1989 (7.04). 42Twelve consecutive years between 1979 and 1991 sit at 6.86 or higher. 43No other stretch comes close.

44The same pattern shows up in song shares. 45From 1979 through 1991 the proportion of charting male-led songs with register 8 or higher sat in a 31-40% band every year, peaking at 40.2% in 1988. 46In 2017 it dropped to 5.8% — the lowest in the dataset. 47In 2019 it was 16.2%.

48By decade, the all-songs average climbs from 5.77 in the 1950s, rises through the 1960s and 1970s, peaks in the 1980s at 6.92, drifts back down through the 1990s and 2000s, and lands at 6.06 in the 2010s — essentially flat with the 1960s. 49This is the U-shape pop critics keep arguing about. 50It is real, it ends in the 1980s, and 2019 does not break it.

55But the top of the chart is a different country

56Five male-led songs hit the Billboard top 10 in 2019. 57Four of them — Sucker (register 9), If I Can't Have You (9), Talk (7), No Guidance (8) — are sung high. 58Only one, Ed Sheeran and Justin Bieber's I Don't Care, sits in the middle of the scale at register 5. 59The mean of those five songs is 7.60.

60What is unusual is not that high-register hits exist. 61They have always existed. 62What is unusual is that in 2019, almost every male-led top-10 hit fits that pattern at the same time, while the rest of the chart does not. 63The 1.65-point gap between top-10 and all-songs in 2019 is more than double the next-largest gap (0.78 in 2015) and a full inversion of 1995, the only year on record where the top-10 average sat below the all-songs average.

64The shape of the trend is also different from the 1980s peak. 65Hair-metal high registers were stratospheric — Cinderella's "Don't Know What You Got" got 9s, Bon Jovi's "Livin' on a Prayer" sits at 8. 66In 2019 there are zero register-10 male-led songs in the dataset. 67The modern top of the chart leans on registers 7, 8, and 9 — very high, but not the screaming top. 68The pop ceiling has lowered slightly even as the floor has dropped through it.

73Why the all-songs average barely moved

74The reason the 2019 all-songs mean is so low is sitting right inside the 248 charting Hot 100 songs from that year. 75108 of them — 44% — were tagged Rap/Hip hop, with a mean register of 4.45. 76Another 86 songs in the dataset score zero on Pandora's "spoken" axis (fully sung) and average 6.31 in register; the 66 songs that score 8-10 (rapped) average 4.14. 77The 2010s' male charting voice was often a low-register rapped delivery. 78That is what holds the average at 5.95.

79In fact, 2017 — peak streaming-era mumble rap — was the lowest-register year in the entire dataset, at 5.7226 on the all-songs series. 80The decade's floor was as historically low as the chart ceiling was historically high. 81They were happening at the same time and on the same chart.

82One useful baseline. 83In 2019, female-led Hot 100 songs averaged register 6.51 — roughly a point and a half above male-led songs (5.12). 84The 2019 male top-10 mean of 7.60 sits a full point above even that female baseline. 85At the very top of the chart, a year of male singing crossed over the year's average female register.

90What the chart wants now

91Two things were true about 2019 pop, and they sat in opposite places. 92Below the top-10, men sang lower than at any point since the late 1950s. 93At the top-10, men sang higher than at any point in the chart's history. 94There has never been a 1.65-point gap before. 95There has never been a 7.6 top-10 average before.

96The Vox Earworm video built on this dataset makes the audible case: the radio sound of the late 2010s — the soft, bedroom-pop, post-Drake, post-Weeknd register — has a falsetto on it more often than not. 97The numbers say something narrower and stranger. 98A high voice is not what defines 2010s pop. 99A high voice is what defines 2010s hits.

100The chart in 2019 was not telling men to sing higher. 101It was telling them: if you want to win, sing higher. 102Most of them did not. 103Five did, and four of those five had to.
