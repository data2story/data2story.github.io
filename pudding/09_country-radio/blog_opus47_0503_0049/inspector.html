14In May 2015, a country-radio consultant named Keith Hill told the trade press that programmers should not stack women's songs together. 15He used a salad analogy: men were the lettuce; women were the tomatoes. 16Eight years and a thousand op-eds later, the rule he described is still on the air.

22At Cincinnati's WUBE-FM, two women's songs followed each other twice. Two times. 23In 6,221 plays across 19 sampled days. 24The men's back-to-back rate at the same station was 1,917 times higher.

26The number that should not exist

27To check whether the gap is just an arithmetic side effect of women being rarer, The Pudding shuffled each station's 24-hour playlist 1,000 times, with no avoidance rule applied, and asked a simple question: how often does the random shuffle produce a women's back-to-back rate as low as the observed one? 28At 18 of 29 stations, the answer was never. 29Across 18,000 randomized days, no shuffle ever drove the women's b2b rate down to where the actual programmers drove it.

30The simple back-of-envelope check is even cleaner. 31If women have an 11% airplay share and slots were random, you would expect a back-to-back rate of about (0.11)² = 1.2% — the chance baseline. 32Every single station in the sample comes in below that baseline. 33The median observed-over-expected ratio is 0.51, meaning the typical station runs at half of chance. 34WUBE-FM runs at 3% of chance. 35The pattern is not under-representation. 36It is under-representation plus active separation.

42Quarantine, not absence

43Inside the day, women's plays are not spread across the dayparts evenly. 4433% of all women's plays in the 2022 sample fall in overnight hours — midnight to 6 a.m. 45— when listenership is at its weekly low. 46Another 21% fall in the evening. 47The morning drive (6-10 a.m.) and afternoon drive (3-7 p.m.), where ratings and ad rates peak, hold only 12% and 15% of women's plays.

48When women DO get back-to-backed, the lopsidedness gets sharper. 49Of the 928 women's back-to-back pairs in the sample, 43% landed in overnights and another 21% in evenings — 63% of women's b2bs concentrated in the slots almost no one is listening. 50Afternoon drive, the most valuable hour of the day for ad sales, holds 9% of women's b2bs.

51The rule is not "play fewer women." The rule is "play them where it doesn't show."

56If you narrow the lens from "women" to "women of color," the pattern stops being a percentage and starts being a list. 57Five women of color received plays in the entire 2022 sample: Tiera Kennedy (13 plays), Runaway June (11), Gloria Estefan, Whitney Houston, and Loretta Lynn (one play each). 5821 of the 29 stations played zero women of color in 2022. 59None of the five appeared back-to-back with another woman of color anywhere in the dataset. 60The most-cited Black woman in country music in the last five years — Mickey Guyton — does not appear in the 2022 logs from these 29 stations at all.

78Three women, thirty-two careers

79Among "current" songs in the 2022 sample — the active-promotion bucket where new artists earn their careers — there are 35 unique women artists. 80The top three of them, Gabby Barrett, Kelsea Ballerini, and Lainey Wilson, account for 52.2% of all current-women plays. 81The top three current men account for 20.3% of current-men plays — across a roster of 158 artists. 82Women's path through country radio in 2022 is not a roster, it is a bottleneck.

83When women DO get back-to-backed, the pairs are usually safe — Recurrent or Gold tracks, not Current ones. 8433% of women's back-to-backs are Gold (older hits like "Before He Cheats"); 45% Recurrent; only 22% Current. 85Two new women's songs in a row remains rare even when the rule is bent.

91Thirty percent of days, zero

92Collapse the 2022 sample to station-days — 29 stations × 19 dates each — and you get 551 single-day playlists. 93166 of them, 30.1%, contain zero back-to-back women's pairs. 94In a 24-hour broadcast day, on a country station, in 2022, the most common outcome is no back-to-back women at all.

95The most striking single day in the dataset is January 28, 2022 at WSM-FM in Nashville — the legendary station that hosts the Grand Ole Opry. 96WSM played 53 women's songs that day, 15.6% of its airtime. 97Zero of them were back-to-back.

98Across all 29 stations, the floor is WUBE-FM Cincinnati at 0.032%; the ceiling is KKGO-FM Los Angeles at 1.27% — and KKGO is an independent station, the only sample that even touches the chance baseline. 99The other 28 stations live in a 0.03-1.0% band that no random shuffle would produce.

What the rule costs

103Women's share of country airplay was 33.1% in 1999. 104By 2015 it was 11.4% — the year of Hill's "tomato" comment. 105By 2022 it averaged about 11% on the 156 Mediabase-tracked stations. 106The trajectory is a half-century slope, with a near-floor at the bottom of it.

107Country radio is not just a listening choice. 108It is the gate that controls chart position, label promotion budgets, festival booking, award eligibility, and tour revenue. 109When women receive a tenth of airplay and a hundred-and-twenty-eighth of back-to-back airplay, every downstream system inherits that floor. 110Brandi Carlile's response to the Pudding piece was less a critique than a question to the format: 111what does that say to a 10-year-old girl about life?

112The 35 women in the 2022 current rotation are not the bench of country music. 113They are the entire active careers the format is willing to invest in. 114Three of them carry the load. 115The other 32 are the tomatoes — and the salad is mostly lettuce.
